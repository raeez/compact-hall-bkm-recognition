\begin{abstract}
Extensions of coherent sheaves provide the geometric input for a compact
Hall algebra. We ask when compatible finite-height comparisons identify
its positive Borcherds subalgebra and extend to a graded or completed
bialgebra. The translation quotient for a K3 surface times an elliptic
curve retains one common torsor and has zero-object carrier $BE$.
Its determinant cross line leads to a conditional normalization of
associator signs for one additive charge.
In the normalized rational smooth spherical sector, we construct the
relative coefficients and identify the actual global Hall product and
direct-sum coproduct in the tensor category generated by an explicit
polynomial crossing. One monoidal Hodge formality comparison retains
the full flag traces and translation action. The degree-zero fibre
quotient is the ordinary polynomial Hopf algebra; ordinary symmetry on
the full carrier and a normalized constant relative retraction are
obstructed. A distinct Euler-corrected coproduct satisfies its
identities in specified localizations.
Over support, the ordered coefficient comparison and external
product construct the actual direct-sum coalgebra, including its
collision maps. Smallness of the full-flag morphism extends the
permutation action across collisions. Its trace identifies the
fixed Hall multiplication with a shuffle sum, giving a supported
Hopf comparison in a specified permutation tensor category.
On a cyclic length-three family, canonical evaluation maps compare
this construction with the proper flag coefficient and the
ordinary decomposition image. The latter retains its ramified
base-change defect and cannot carry the generic proper trace.
For a fixed ordinary even Borcherds presentation, we prove finite-height
recognition in the total-height symmetric monoidal category.
We reconstruct graded bialgebras from towers with supplied restriction
isomorphisms, and identify their specified height completions without
finite-dimensionality assumptions. Rectangular coproduct targets require
the sum of the two output bounds.
An explicit polynomial current Hopf algebra has a split reverse quotient
onto its spherical subalgebra and a nonzero kernel in every positive
window. Thus spherical recognition does not identify the full carrier.
For dg enveloping algebras, signed PBW symmetrization identifies
primitives and commutes with cohomology. We contrast this with explicit
failures for general differential graded Hopf algebras and quotients,
and construct the ordered bar comparison with Lie chains.
For a quadratic surface algebra, we compute the full space of
differential classes and classify every central cocycle of ordinary
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ currents with fixed trace pairing. A $22$-dimensional
nonzero quadratic coefficient space has $231$ central directions; the
positive-current specialization has explicit smaller images and kernels.
Broader reduced critical coefficients, independent field or
Ran-space realizations, full-current recognition, and state or
amplitude comparisons retain their separate hypotheses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Extension correspondences and critical coefficients}
\label{sec:compact-geometry}

An extension combines two sheaves while retaining how they are joined.
A Hall product must carry this extension data into its coefficients.
The comparison with a Borcherds algebra therefore starts with the moduli
stack and its extension correspondence.
The algebraic question is then whether finite-height comparisons preserve
all bialgebra operations compatibly, so that they determine one graded
comparison and its completion. A second question remains even after such
a comparison: does it recognize the whole Hall carrier or only the
subalgebra generated by the chosen root classes?

In the normalized spherical sector, the proper flag correspondence
also determines the symmetry required by the Hall product.
Its permutation action extends across support collisions and
compares the product with the actual direct-sum coproduct.
The cyclic family then distinguishes this supported Hopf
comparison from an evaluation into scalar coefficients: its
proper trace and strict-splitting image have different extension
properties.

Throughout, $\kk$ is a field of characteristic zero.
The geometric constructions take place over $\mathbb C$.
Fix a finite set $I$ and the charge lattice
\[
 Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb Z\alpha_i,\qquad
 Q_+=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\alpha_i.
\]

Let $X$ be a smooth projective complex Calabi--Yau threefold.  Choose an
extension-closed subcategory $\mathcal A\subset\operatorname{Coh}(X)$,
closed under direct sums, whose objects form an open derived substack of
the derived moduli stack of perfect complexes on $X$.  Choose a charge map
\[
 \operatorname{cl}:K_0(\mathcal A)\longrightarrow Q
\]
which is locally constant in families and whose effective classes lie in
$Q_+$.  Assume that the Euler form factors through an antisymmetric
bilinear form $\chi:Q\times Q\to\mathbb Z$:
\[
 \chi(\operatorname{cl}E,\operatorname{cl}F)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^r
   \dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Ext}^r(E,F).
\]
Let $\mathfrak M_\beta$ be the open-and-closed derived substack of objects
of charge $\beta$.  With the convention that the first factor is the
subobject, the stack
$\mathfrak E_{\beta,\gamma}$ of short exact sequences
\[
 0\longrightarrow E_\beta\longrightarrow E
  \longrightarrow E_\gamma\longrightarrow0
\]
has the typed correspondence
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak M_\beta\times\mathfrak M_\gamma
 \xleftarrow{\ p_{\beta,\gamma}\ }
 \mathfrak E_{\beta,\gamma}
 \xrightarrow{\ q_{\beta,\gamma}\ }
 \mathfrak M_{\beta+\gamma}.                                 \label{hallcorr}
\end{equation}

The $(-1)$-shifted symplectic structure supplies, after a choice of
orientation $o$, Donaldson--Thomas coefficient complexes
$\mathcal P_{\beta,o}$.  A prospective cohomological carrier is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_\beta
   =\mathbb H^\bullet(\mathfrak M_\beta,\mathcal P_{\beta,o}),
 \qquad
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_+}\mathcal H_\beta.         \label{hallcarrier}
\end{equation}
For a moduli-like stack whose classical truncation is a quasi-separated
Artin stack with affine stabilizers and is $\Theta$-reductive, and for which
the unit inclusion is closed, a \emph{strong orientation datum} contains an
orientation isomorphism at the zero object and its two unit coherences, as
well as the compatibility with direct sums and the coherence for triple
direct sums.  It yields maps of the form
\begin{equation}
 m_{\beta,\gamma}:
 \mathcal H_\beta\otimes\mathcal H_\gamma
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal H_{\beta+\gamma}[\chi(\beta,\gamma)],               \label{hallproduct}
\end{equation}
where $\chi$ is the antisymmetric Euler pairing.  The shift in
\eqref{hallproduct} is part of the structure: componentwise shifts cannot
remove a nonzero antisymmetric bilinear degree.  Indeed, replacing
$\mathcal H_\beta$ by $\mathcal H_\beta[s(\beta)]$ changes the degree to
\[
 \chi_s(\beta,\gamma)=\chi(\beta,\gamma)+s(\beta)+s(\gamma)
 -s(\beta+\gamma),\qquad
 \chi_s(\beta,\gamma)-\chi_s(\gamma,\beta)=2\chi(\beta,\gamma).
\]
Thus the product must be placed in its specified charge-twisted monoidal
category.

Kontsevich and Soibelman construct this structure for quivers with
potential~\cite[\S7]{KontsevichSoibelman2011}.  In the general
three-Calabi--Yau setting, the hypotheses above and strong orientation data
give associativity
by~\cite[Theorem A, Definition 8.4, Corollary 8.8]{KinjoParkSafronov2026}.
Joyce and Upmeier construct canonical orientation data for smooth
projective Calabi--Yau threefolds~\cite[Theorem 4.4]{JoyceUpmeier2021}.
They identify the obstruction to a strong lift as a class in
$H^3(K^{\mathrm{semi}}_0(X),\mathbb Z/2)$ and leave its vanishing
open~\cite[Theorem 3.10, Question 3.11]{JoyceUpmeier2021}.
Without that coherence, the product is associative only up to choices of
sign~\cite[Remark 8.7]{KinjoParkSafronov2026}; the compactly supported
construction of~\cite[Theorem 7.22]{Descombes2025} likewise assumes a
strong orientation datum and records the unresolved lift in
\cite[Remark 7.23]{Descombes2025}. That construction gives a compactly
supported coalgebra, whose dual carries the Hall product; it does not
supply a compatible second operation making the same carrier a bialgebra.
Thus
\eqref{hallcorr} and \eqref{hallcarrier} do not yet furnish the required
associative compact Hall algebra.

\begin{remark}[The two-Calabi--Yau comparison]
For Gieseker semistable coherent sheaves of fixed normalized Hilbert
polynomial on a polarized K3 surface, the sign-twisted two-Calabi--Yau
BPS algebra admits an enveloping-algebra description
\cite[\S1.4.1, Theorems 1.1--1.2]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}.
The theorem uses rational coefficients and cohomologically graded
generator spaces. Its framework includes the proper representable
extension projection, the resolution and three-term presentation
hypotheses, a separated good moduli scheme with finite-type components,
a finite direct-sum morphism, and right two-Calabi--Yau structures on
finite collections of simple objects. Associativity of the Hall product
is also a hypothesis: these are Assumptions 1--5 and 7 in
\cite[\S\S4.2--4.3, 6.1, 7.7.3]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}.

The surjective charge map has finite fibres and takes values in a monoid
embedded in a lattice. Write $(\ ,\ )_{\mathcal C}$ for the symmetric
Euler form of the ambient two-Calabi--Yau category $\mathcal C$; it
factors through this monoid.
For a bilinear $\psi$ satisfying
\[
 \psi(a,b)+\psi(b,a)=(a,b)_{\mathcal C}\pmod2,
\]
the product is twisted by $(-1)^{\psi(a,b)}$
\cite[\S\S4.4, 6.3.1]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}.
The resulting BPS algebra is the enveloping algebra of the positive
generalized Kac--Moody Lie algebra. The proof reduces to half Ext-quivers
and an induction on quivers with dimension vectors
\cite[\S7.7.2]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}.

The BPS algebra injects into the full cohomological Hall algebra
\cite[Proposition 6.4]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}; twisting both
multiplications preserves this inclusion. The splitting used in that
proof is a splitting of underlying complexes, not an algebra retraction.
The additional positive determinant line of Assumption 6 gives the
full-CoHA PBW comparisons as isomorphisms of complexes or graded mixed
Hodge structures. They are explicitly not algebra morphisms
\cite[\S4.6, Theorem 1.7, Corollary 1.8 and the following paragraph,
\S8.3.2]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}.
These results therefore do not supply the three-Calabi--Yau algebra or
bialgebra surjection required in Proposition~\ref{reverse-quotient}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Translation quotients retain one torsor}

For $X=S\times E$, where $S$ is a projective K3 surface and $E$
is an elliptic curve, translation changes the elliptic origin.
An extension still depends on the relative positions of its endpoint
sheaves. Quotienting the two origins independently loses this information.

Work in the fppf topology, with 2-fibre products of stacks.
The stack $BE$ classifies right $E$-torsors.
For a stack $M$ with a left $E$-action, an object of $[M/E](T)$
is a right torsor $P\to T$ and an equivariant map $m:P\to M$,
with $m(pe)=e^{-1}m(p)$.
For stack-valued maps this description includes their descent
isomorphisms. Forgetting $m$ gives $[M/E]\to BE$.

\begin{proposition}\label{common-torsor}
For $E$-stacks $M,N$, there is a natural equivalence
\[
 [(M\times N)/E_{\mathrm{diag}}]
 \simeq [M/E]\times_{BE}[N/E].
\]
These equivalences commute with regrouping any finite number of factors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An object on the right consists of $(P,m)$, $(Q,n)$, and a torsor
isomorphism $\alpha:P\to Q$. Send it to $(P,m,n\alpha)$.
Conversely, use the same torsor twice and its identity isomorphism.
For a morphism, the relation $b\alpha=\alpha'a$ determines the second
torsor map $b$ from the first map $a$.
The constructions are inverse up to their specified isomorphisms
and commute with base change.
For several factors, every regrouping gives one torsor with the same
equivariant maps. This also proves the regrouping coherence.
\end{proof}

For example, translation gives $[E/E]\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$,
whereas
\[
 [(E\times E)/E_{\mathrm{diag}}]\simeq E,\qquad
 (x,y)\longmapsto y-x.
\]
Thus the fibre product over $BE$ retains the relative translation.
The ordinary product of the two quotient stacks is a point.

Let $\mathfrak M$ be the stack of coherent sheaves on $X$, with families
flat over the parameter scheme. Put $\mathfrak Q=[\mathfrak M/E]$.
An object over $T$ is a torsor $P\to T$ and a flat coherent sheaf
on $X_P=P\times^E X\simeq S\times P$.
Choose an ample line bundle on $X$.
Hilbert polynomials are invariant under the connected translation group,
so they define substacks $\mathfrak Q_a$ independently of the local
trivialization of $P$.

Let $\mathfrak H_{a,b}$ classify a common torsor and a sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow B
 \longrightarrow0
\]
on $X_P$, with flat terms of Hilbert polynomials $a,a+b,b$.
The correspondence is
\begin{equation}\label{relative-hallcorr}
 \mathfrak Q_a\times_{BE}\mathfrak Q_b
 \xleftarrow{\ p\ }\mathfrak H_{a,b}
 \xrightarrow{\ q\ }\mathfrak Q_{a+b}.
\end{equation}
Here $p$ records the endpoint sheaves on their common torsor, and $q$
records the middle sheaf.

\begin{proposition}\label{relative-hall-flags}
The correspondences \eqref{relative-hallcorr} define a coherently
associative unital Hall object in the category of spans of stacks over
$BE$, with tensor product $\times_{BE}$.
Its unit is
\[
 BE\xleftarrow{\operatorname{id}}BE
       \xrightarrow{0}\mathfrak Q.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both triple products classify a common torsor and a flag
$0\subset A\subset D\subset F$.
For the bracketing that first forms $F/A$, recover $D$ as the inverse
image in $F$ of the prescribed subobject of $F/A$.
Conversely, a flag gives both pairs of short exact sequences.
These constructions preserve the endpoint sheaves, the middle sheaf,
and base change. They identify the two composite spans.

For any number of factors, every parenthesization reconstructs the
same full flag by inclusions and inverse images.
The resulting identifications preserve each subobject and specified
quotient, so their composites agree.
This gives the associativity coherences.
The zero sheaf has no sheaf automorphisms, but retains its translation
torsor. Its quotient is therefore $BE$.
Inserting a zero step in a flag gives the two unit identities and their
coherences.
\end{proof}

An $E$-invariant extension-closed sector inherits this construction
when its families and admissible sequences satisfy descent and base
change. Properness of the restricted map $q$ remains a separate
condition.

A cohomological realization of \eqref{relative-hallcorr} must specify
coefficients, the pullback or Gysin map along $p$, pushforward along $q$,
their shifts, and coherent base change on the flag diagrams.
Its tensor comparison must realize the fibre product over $BE$.
Applying such a monoidal realization to
Proposition~\ref{relative-hall-flags} gives an associative unital algebra
in its target category.

The zero-object stabilizer is the elliptic curve $E$, which is not
affine. Consequently, the affine-stabilizer hypothesis of the absolute
critical Hall theorem cited above fails for $\mathfrak Q$.
The geometric flag construction alone does not supply a relative critical
coefficient theory over $BE$.
Removing translation or trace directions also requires a reduced
coefficient complex, with its own duality and extension comparisons.
Section~\ref{sec:spherical-hall} constructs such a specified coefficient
system directly in the smooth spherical sector, with its $BE$ unit.

\subsection{The orientation cross line}

Choose an $E$-invariant holomorphic volume form on $X=S\times E$.
For perfect families on $\pi:X_P\to T$, suppose the complexes
\[
 C(A,B)=R\pi_*\operatorname{RHom}_{X_P}(A,B)
\]
are perfect over $T$.
Write $L(A,B)=\det C(A,B)$ and $D(A)=L(A,A)$.
The determinant convention is
$\det C=\bigotimes_i(\det C^i)^{(-1)^i}$, with parity
$\chi(C)\bmod2$.

Relative Serre duality gives
$C(B,A)\simeq C(A,B)^\vee[-3]$.
Duality and the odd shift each invert the determinant line, hence
$L(B,A)\simeq L(A,B)$.
For an extension $0\to A\to F\to B\to0$, determinant additivity
in the two variables gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 D(F)
 &\simeq L(B,A)\otimes D(A)\otimes D(B)\otimes L(A,B)\\
 &\simeq D(A)\otimes D(B)\otimes L(A,B)^{\otimes2}.
 \end{split}
\]
The second line uses the determinant symmetry, including its parity
sign. This is the extension identity of
\cite[equations (4.3)--(4.5)]{JoyceUpmeier2021}.

An orientation line $o_A$ is a square root of $D(A)$.
Extension compatibility requires an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{orientation-cross-map}
 o_A\otimes o_B\otimes L(A,B)\longrightarrow o_F
\end{equation}
whose square is the determinant comparison.
For three successive quotients, both bracketings have cross factors
$L(A,B)\otimes L(A,C)\otimes L(B,C)$.
Agreement of their squares leaves a possible $\mu_2$ discrepancy.
Strong orientation data require agreement of the square-root maps
themselves, together with unit coherence.
On the quotient they also require compatible $E$-linearizations.
The displayed calculation concerns unreduced complexes.
It does not construct these data for a reduced critical theory.

\subsection{Associator signs for one additive charge}

The possible sign between two orientation comparisons has an elementary
normalization when it depends only on one additive charge. The charge
monoid in the next proposition is $\mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ with
addition. It is independent of cohomological degree and parity.

\begin{proposition}[Associator signs for one charge]
\label{one-charge-associator}
Let $c:\mathbb N^3\to\mathbb F_2$ be normalized, meaning that it vanishes
whenever an argument is zero. Suppose that, for all $n,m,k,l\in\mathbb N$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 c(m,k,l)+c(n+m,k,l)+c(n,m+k,l)\hspace{2em}\\
 {}+c(n,m,k+l)+c(n,m,k)=0.
 \end{split}\label{sign-cocycle}
\end{equation}
Define the binary cochain
\[
 b(n,0)=b(n,1)=0,\qquad
 b(n,m)=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}c(n,j,1)\quad(m\geq2).
\]
Then $b(n,0)=b(0,m)=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 c(n,m,k)=b(m,k)+b(n+m,k)+b(n,m+k)+b(n,m).
 \label{sign-coboundary}
\end{equation}
Consequently binary comparison maps with scalar associator
$(-1)^{c(n,m,k)}$ become associative after multiplication by
$(-1)^{b(n,m)}$. Both unit comparisons are unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All sums in the proof take values in $\mathbb F_2$.
Normalization of $c$ gives $b(0,m)=0$; the other unit value is part of
the definition. Let $\delta b$ denote the right side of
\eqref{sign-coboundary}. It is normalized, and the defining sum gives
\[
 (\delta b)(n,m,1)=b(n,m+1)+b(n,m)=c(n,m,1).
\]
Expanding the four-variable coboundary of $\delta b$ gives zero:
each value of $b$ occurs twice. Thus $c'=c+\delta b$ is a normalized
cocycle, with $c'(n,m,1)=0$.
Apply~\eqref{sign-cocycle} to $c'$ with $l=1$. The first three terms
vanish, leaving $c'(n,m,k+1)=c'(n,m,k)$.
Starting at $k=0$, normalization gives $c'=0$ for every $k$.
This proves~\eqref{sign-coboundary} for all triples.

Rephasing the two binary maps in each parenthesization changes the
associator exponent by $\delta b$, so the new exponent is zero.
For a comparison with the unit on the right the sign is
$(-1)^{b(n,0)}=1$; with the unit on the left it is
$(-1)^{b(0,n)}=1$. Hence both unit comparisons remain fixed.
\end{proof}

For example, $c(n,m,k)=nmk\bmod2$ is normalized and satisfies
\eqref{sign-cocycle}: expanding the five terms cancels every monomial
in pairs. The cochain is
\[
 b(n,m)=n\binom m2\pmod2.
\]
The identity
$\binom{m+k}{2}=\binom m2+\binom k2+mk$
then gives $\delta b(n,m,k)=nmk$ directly.
Here $b(1,2)=1$ while $b(1,0)=0$.
If this same cochain is forced to depend only on $m\bmod2$, the function
$b(n,m\bmod2)$ is identically zero. Its coboundary at $(1,1,1)$ is zero,
whereas $c(1,1,1)=1$. The normalization on additive $\mathbb N$ is valid;
it does not descend by replacing additive charge with parity.

For orientation lines, application of the proposition requires actual
descent of the scalar associator to charge triples and local constancy
on each such triple. The binary comparisons must already have normalized
unit comparisons, and their discrepancy must satisfy
\eqref{sign-cocycle} on quadruple direct sums. On the translation quotient
these data must also descend with the required $E$-equivariance.
Under these hypotheses the proposition corrects the binary signs.
It constructs neither reduced critical coefficients nor a geometric
coproduct, and it does not establish these descent hypotheses or the
strong orientation lift for an arbitrary moduli stack.

\section{Scalar denominators and Lie brackets}

For a positively graded vector space $V=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}V_n$ with finite-dimensional
components, write
\[
 D_V(t)=\prod_{n\geq1}(1-t^n)^{\dim_\kk V_n}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[The scalar shadow is not faithful]\label{scalar-not-faithful}
There are nonisomorphic positively graded Lie algebras $L$ and $L_0$ for
which
\[
 D_L(t)=D_{L_0}(t)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L)=\operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L_0).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $L$ have basis $x,y$ in degree one and $z$ in degree two, with
$[x,y]=z$ and $z$ central.  Let $L_0$ have the same graded vector space and
zero bracket.  Then
\[
 [L,L]=\kk z,\qquad [L_0,L_0]=0,
\]
so the two Lie algebras are not isomorphic.  Their common scalar
denominator is
\[
 D_L(t)=D_{L_0}(t)=(1-t)^2(1-t^2).
\]
Theorem~\ref{sp:pbw} gives
\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L)
 =\operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L_0)
 =\frac{1}{(1-t)^2(1-t^2)}.
\]
Nevertheless, $U(L_0)$ is commutative, whereas $xy-yx=z\neq0$ in $U(L)$.
\end{proof}

Thus graded dimensions determine neither the Lie bracket nor the
associative product of its enveloping algebra.  They also do not determine
the correspondences, orientations, or functorial maps from which a Hall
product is formed.

\section{The positive Borcherds presentation}

A comparison on generators requires a presentation of the target
Lie algebra. Use the finite index set $I$ of the charge lattice.
An \emph{integral symmetrizable
Borcherds--Cartan matrix} is an integral matrix
$A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $a_{ii}=2$ or $a_{ii}\leq0$;
\item $a_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$, and $a_{ij}=0$ if and only if
      $a_{ji}=0$;
\item there are positive integers $d_i$ for which
      $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$.
\end{enumerate}
Put
\[
 \Ire=\{i\in I:a_{ii}=2\},\qquad
 \Iim=\{i\in I:a_{ii}\leq0\}.
\]
The symmetric form on
$Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb Z\alpha_i$ is
$(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)=d_i a_{ij}$.  Thus the simple roots indexed by
$\Ire$ have positive square and those indexed by $\Iim$ have nonpositive
square.  We work in ordinary vector spaces: every simple generator is even,
and no Lie-superalgebra signs occur.

The height of a charge is
\[
 \htop\left(\sum_i n_i\alpha_i\right)=\sum_i n_i.
\]
Let $\mathfrak F$ be the free $Q_+$-graded Lie algebra on generators $e_i$
of degree $\alpha_i$.  Let $R_A\subset\mathfrak F$ be the homogeneous ideal
generated by
\begin{align}
 (\operatorname{ad}e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j&=0
 &&(i\in\Ire,\ j\neq i),                                    \label{serre}\\
 [e_i,e_j]&=0
 &&(i\neq j,\ a_{ij}=0).                                    \label{orthogonal}
\end{align}
The positive Borcherds Lie algebra considered here is
\[
 \mathfrak n_+(A)=\mathfrak F/R_A.
\]
These are the normalized positive relations in Borcherds'
presentation~\cite[\S1]{Borcherds1988}.  A simple imaginary generator
$e_i$, $i\in\Iim$, imposes no relation
$(\operatorname{ad}e_i)^m e_j=0$ unless the orthogonality relation
\eqref{orthogonal} applies.  A relation based at a real index $j$ may still
constrain $e_i$.  For example, for
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
the relation based at $1$ is
$(\operatorname{ad}e_1)^2e_2=0$, and there is no converse Serre relation
based at $2$.

Finiteness of $I$ implies that every $Q_+$-homogeneous component of
$\mathfrak F$, and hence of $\mathfrak n_+(A)$, is finite-dimensional.

\begin{remark}[What the positive presentation does not recover]
The matrix $A$ is fixed input.  Its positive Lie algebra need not determine
$A$: the rank-one matrices $(0)$ and $(-2)$ both give
$\mathfrak n_+=\kk e$.  Recognition of $\mathfrak n_+(A)$ therefore does
not recover the Cartan matrix, the Cartan and negative summands, or the full
Borcherds algebra.  The present formulation also assumes a finite set of
independent simple charges, one even generator for each charge.  Charged
generator spaces, odd simple roots, and Borcherds--Bozec generators in
multiple degrees are outside its scope.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Recognition of the positive Lie algebra}

The height category now retains exactly the operations needed for a
finite comparison. We return to ordinary even generators and the fixed
positive presentation of $\mathfrak n_+(A)$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-height Lie recognition]                \label{lie-recognition}
Let
\[
 L=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}}L_\beta
\]
be a $Q_+$-graded Lie algebra with finite-dimensional homogeneous pieces.
Suppose that elements $x_i\in L_{\alpha_i}$ generate $L$ and satisfy
\eqref{serre} and \eqref{orthogonal}, with $e_i$ replaced by $x_i$.
Then there is a unique surjective graded Lie homomorphism
\begin{equation}
 \pi:\mathfrak n_+(A)\longrightarrow L,\qquad e_i\longmapsto x_i.
                                                               \label{pi}
\end{equation}
If, for every $\beta\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$ with
$\htop(\beta)\leq N$,
\begin{equation}
 \dim_\kk L_\beta=\dim_\kk\mathfrak n_+(A)_\beta,             \label{dimensions}
\end{equation}
then
\[
 \tau_{\leq N}\pi:\tau_{\leq N}\mathfrak n_+(A)
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\tau_{\leq N}L
\]
is an isomorphism of Lie algebra objects in
$\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\leq N}U(\pi):\tau_{\leq N}U(\mathfrak n_+(A))
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\tau_{\leq N}U(L)               \label{pbwiso}
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism of bialgebra objects in that category.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assignment $e_i\mapsto x_i$ gives a surjection
$\mathfrak F\twoheadrightarrow L$.  The displayed relations place $R_A$ in
its kernel, so it factors through the unique surjection \eqref{pi}.  For
each $\beta$ of height at most $N$, the map $\pi_\beta$ is a surjection
between finite-dimensional spaces of the same dimension and hence is an
isomorphism.

Give both enveloping algebras the PBW filtration.  The associated graded map
of $U(\pi)$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}(\pi):
 \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak n_+(A))\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Sym}(L).
\]
In a $Q_+$-degree $\beta$ of height at most $N$, every symmetric monomial
uses only Lie components of height at most $N$; hence
$\operatorname{Sym}(\pi)_\beta$ is an isomorphism.  The PBW filtration in a
fixed positive height is finite, so $U(\pi)_\beta$ is an isomorphism.
Functoriality of the standard primitive coproduct makes $U(\pi)$ a
bialgebra map, and strong monoidality of $\tau_{\leq N}$ gives
\eqref{pbwiso}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The dimensions in \eqref{dimensions} do not create a bracket.  The
relations and the chosen generators first produce the surjection \eqref{pi};
the dimensions only show that its kernel vanishes through height $N$.
\end{remark}

\section{Associative recognition and reverse quotients}
\label{sec:associative-comparison}

A recognized Lie algebra acts through its enveloping algebra.
Its image in a Hall algebra can satisfy additional associative
relations. The comparison must therefore identify that image and
its kernel separately.

Let $\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_+}\mathcal H_\beta$ be a unital
$Q_+$-graded associative algebra, and let
$x_i\in\mathcal H_{\alpha_i}$.  Denote by $L\subset\mathcal H$ the Lie
subalgebra generated by the $x_i$ under the commutator, and by
$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}\subset\mathcal H$ the unital associative
subalgebra generated by the same elements.  The inclusion
$L\hookrightarrow\mathcal H$ induces an algebra map
\[
 U(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}},
\]
but Lie recognition alone says nothing about its kernel.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-height associative recognition]    \label{associative-recognition}
If the $x_i$ satisfy \eqref{serre} and \eqref{orthogonal}, there is a unique
surjective graded algebra map
\begin{equation}
 \Phi:U(\mathfrak n_+(A))\twoheadrightarrow
       \mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}},\qquad e_i\longmapsto x_i.    \label{Phi}
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}
 \dim_\kk\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}_\beta
 =\dim_\kk U(\mathfrak n_+(A))_\beta
 \qquad\bigl(\htop(\beta)\leq N\bigr),                        \label{associative-dimensions}
\end{equation}
then $\tau_{\leq N}\Phi$ is an isomorphism of associative algebra objects
in $\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$.  If $\mathcal H$ is a graded bialgebra and
the $x_i$ are primitive, then $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}$ is a graded
sub-bialgebra, $\Phi$ is a bialgebra map, and the same conclusion holds for
bialgebra objects.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relations give a Lie map
$\mathfrak n_+(A)\to\mathcal H$ with image $L$.  Its enveloping map has
image exactly the unital algebra generated by the $x_i$, proving
\eqref{Phi}.  Under \eqref{associative-dimensions}, every homogeneous
component through height $N$ is a surjection between finite-dimensional
spaces of equal dimension.  If the $x_i$ are primitive, the standard
coproduct on $U(\mathfrak n_+(A))$ and the coproduct on $\mathcal H$ agree
on the algebra generators, which proves the bialgebra assertion.
\end{proof}

For $N\geq1$, put
\[
 B_N=\tau_{\leq N}U(\mathfrak n_+(A)),\qquad
 S_N=\tau_{\leq N}\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}},\qquad
 H_N=\tau_{\leq N}\mathcal H,
\]
and write $\jmath_N:S_N\hookrightarrow H_N$ for the inclusion and
$\Phi_N=\tau_{\leq N}\Phi$.

\begin{proposition}[The reverse quotient is additional data] \label{reverse-quotient}
Assume the hypotheses producing \eqref{Phi}.  If there is a unital graded
algebra morphism in $\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$
\begin{equation}
 \rho_N:H_N\longrightarrow B_N,\qquad \rho_N(x_i)=e_i,       \label{rhoN}
\end{equation}
then $\rho_N$ is surjective and
\begin{equation}
 \rho_N\jmath_N\Phi_N=\operatorname{id}_{B_N}.              \label{splitPhi}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\Phi_N:B_N\to S_N$ is an isomorphism with inverse
$\rho_N|_{S_N}$, and
\[
 H_N/\ker\rho_N\cong B_N.
\]
The map $\rho_N$ is an isomorphism precisely when $H_N=S_N$.  In
particular, this holds if all homogeneous components are finite-dimensional
and
\[
 \dim_\kk(H_N)_\beta=\dim_\kk(B_N)_\beta
 \qquad\bigl(\htop(\beta)\leq N\bigr).
\]
If $H_N$ is a bialgebra object, the $x_i$ are primitive, and $\rho_N$ is a
bialgebra morphism, then $S_N$ is a sub-bialgebra, $\Phi_N$ and its inverse
are bialgebra isomorphisms, and $H_N/\ker\rho_N\cong B_N$ as bialgebras.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The composite in \eqref{splitPhi} fixes the unit and every generator $e_i$
of $B_N$, hence is the identity.  It follows at once that $\rho_N$ is
surjective and that $\Phi_N$ is injective.  Proposition
\ref{associative-recognition} makes $\Phi_N$ surjective, so its left inverse
$\rho_N|_{S_N}$ is its inverse.  For $h\in H_N$,
\[
 h-\jmath_N\Phi_N\rho_N(h)\in\ker\rho_N,
\]
and $S_N\cap\ker\rho_N=0$.  Hence $\ker\rho_N=0$ is equivalent to
$H_N=S_N$.  The dimension criterion makes each surjective homogeneous
component of $\rho_N$ injective.  Under the final hypotheses,
$S_N$ is a sub-bialgebra and all the maps in \eqref{splitPhi} preserve the
bialgebra operations.
\end{proof}

If $T_N$ is the free unital algebra object on the simple generators and
$I_{B,N}$ and $I_{S,N}$ are the kernels of its maps to $B_N$ and $S_N$,
then $\Phi_N$ records the inclusion
\[
 I_{B,N}\subseteq I_{S,N}.
\]
A reverse map fixing the generators requires the opposite inclusion.
Thus neither \eqref{Phi} nor a scalar denominator constructs \eqref{rhoN}.
PBW and a componentwise dimension equality can construct the inverse on
$S_N$, but extending that inverse to $H_N$ still requires generation or an
independent splitting.  On the spherical subalgebra, the existence of
\eqref{rhoN} forces $I_{B,N}=I_{S,N}$.

\begin{remark}[Lie PBW is not Hall PBW]
For $A=(0)$, take
\[
 \mathcal H=\kk[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 \deg\varepsilon=\alpha,\qquad x=\varepsilon.
\]
Then $L=\kk\varepsilon\cong\mathfrak n_+(A)$, so
Theorem~\ref{lie-recognition} recognizes the Lie algebra in every height.
Nevertheless,
\[
 U(\mathfrak n_+(A))=\kk[e]\longrightarrow\mathcal H,\qquad
 e\longmapsto\varepsilon
\]
has kernel $(e^2)$.  Thus \eqref{dimensions} cannot replace the
associative dimension comparison \eqref{associative-dimensions}.  Moreover,
for $N\geq2$ there is no map \eqref{rhoN} sending $\varepsilon$ to $e$,
since it would send $\varepsilon^2=0$ to the nonzero element $e^2\in B_N$.
To recognize all of $\mathcal H$, one must also prove
$\mathcal H=\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}$; this fails whenever, for example,
$\mathcal H_0$ contains more than the unit.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A reverse quotient with a nonzero kernel]
\label{current-quotient-example}
Take the rank-one datum $A=(2)$, so that
$\mathfrak n_+(A)=\kk e$ and $B=U(\mathfrak n_+(A))=\kk[e]$.
Let $\mathfrak a=\bigoplus_{r\geq0}\kk e_r$ be an abelian Lie algebra,
with every $e_r$ even and of charge $\alpha$. Its enveloping Hopf algebra is
\[
 C=\kk[e_0,e_1,e_2,\ldots],\qquad
 \Delta(e_r)=e_r\otimes1+1\otimes e_r,\qquad
 \varepsilon(e_r)=0,\qquad S(e_r)=-e_r.
\]
Its unit is the constant polynomial $1$.
One may additionally give $e_r$ cohomological degree $2r$ and set $d=0$;
all the following maps preserve that grading if $e$ has degree zero.
This extra grading is not needed for ordinary recognition.

The formulas
\begin{equation}
 \Phi:B\longrightarrow C,\quad e\longmapsto e_0,
 \qquad
 \rho:C\longrightarrow B,\quad
 e_0\longmapsto e,\quad e_r\longmapsto0\ (r>0)
 \label{current-retraction}
\end{equation}
extend uniquely to unital algebra maps. They preserve coproduct and
counit on every primitive generator, hence on every polynomial, and
commute with the stated antipodes. Thus they are Hopf maps.
Since $\rho\Phi=1_B$, the spherical subalgebra $C^{\mathrm{sph}}=\kk[e_0]$
is a split copy of $B$.

Every polynomial involves only finitely many generators. The monomials
containing a positive-index generator span the kernel of evaluation at
$e_1=e_2=\cdots=0$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 I_C:=\ker\rho=(e_1,e_2,\ldots),\qquad
 C=\kk[e_0]\oplus I_C
 \label{current-kernel}
\end{equation}
as graded vector spaces; $I_C$ is a Hopf ideal.
Strong monoidality gives compatible finite bialgebra maps
$\Phi_N:\tau_{\leq N}B\to\tau_{\leq N}C$ and
$\rho_N:\tau_{\leq N}C\to\tau_{\leq N}B$, with
$\rho_N\Phi_N=1$ and
\[
 (\ker\rho_N)_{n\alpha}
 =\operatorname{span}_{\kk}\{
 e_{r_1}\cdots e_{r_n}:\text{some }r_j>0\},\qquad 0\leq n\leq N.
\]
For $n=0$ this span is zero. For every $N\geq1$ it contains
$e_1\neq0$, so the full carrier is larger than its spherical subalgebra
in every positive window. Each $C_{n\alpha}$ with $n>0$ is
infinite-dimensional; the splitting uses no dimension comparison.

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak a$ is the positive abelian current algebra
$\kk e\otimes\kk[t]$, with $e_r=e\otimes t^r$.
Evaluation at $t=0$ induces $\rho$, and inclusion of constant currents
induces $\Phi$. These are the only current operations needed here.
The construction makes no assertion about negative or Cartan modes or
central extensions of a full current Lie algebra. It is an algebraic
model; a compact Hall realization would still have to identify its
carrier, product, coproduct and geometric gradings.
For example, sending $e_0$ to the geometric charge-one constant
and $e_1$ to its Chern class $z$ would not preserve this commuting
product: equation~\eqref{gh:binary-products} gives
$[z,1]_*=-2\delta_{12}$.
The geometric quotient in Corollary~\ref{gh:fibre-quotient} is
constructed from its own product and direct-sum maps.
\end{remark}

\section{Compatible height completion}

A comparison in every finite height determines a completed map only
when the comparisons agree under truncation.
The relevant topology and tensor product can be stated directly.

For a nonnegatively $Q_+$-graded object $V$ in any of the three base
categories of Section~\ref{sec:height-tensor}, set
\[
 F^{N+1}V=\bigoplus_{\htop(\beta)>N}V_\beta,\qquad
 \widehat V=\varprojlim_N V/F^{N+1}V
            =\prod_{\beta\in Q_+}V_\beta.
\]
The transition maps are the projections in \eqref{transition}.  The tensor
product appropriate to this topology is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V\mathbin{\widehat\otimes_{\mathrm{ht}}}\widehat W
 =
 \prod_{\beta\in Q_+}
 \left(\bigoplus_{\gamma+\delta=\beta}
 V_\gamma\otimes W_\delta\right).                            \label{completed-tensor}
\end{equation}
Each inner sum is finite because $I$ is finite.
The kernels of the projections $\widehat V\to\tau_{\leq N}V$ form a
basis of neighborhoods of zero; every homogeneous piece is discrete.
This is the height topology. For complexes, all products and inverse
limits here are taken degreewise, while each $V_\gamma\otimes W_\delta$
is the ordinary tensor complex. There is no completion in cohomological
degree. Explicitly, the tensor complex uses sum totalization:
\[
 (V_\gamma\otimes W_\delta)^j
 =\bigoplus_{p+q=j}V_\gamma^p\otimes W_\delta^q.
\]

\subsection{Completion of comparisons}

The reconstruction theorem turns compatible finite comparisons into
continuous maps. The following consequences apply it to recognition and
to the reverse quotient.

\begin{proposition}[Formal height completion]                 \label{completion}
Let $f:V\to W$ be a charge-preserving morphism in the chosen base
category. If
$\tau_{\leq N}f$ is an isomorphism for every $N$, then $f$ is a
componentwise isomorphism and extends to a continuous isomorphism
\[
 \widehat f:\widehat V\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\widehat W.
\]
If $V$ and $W$ are graded Lie algebras, associative algebras, or bialgebras
and $f$ is a morphism of the corresponding kind, then $\widehat f$ respects
the continuous operations; for bialgebras the completed coproduct takes
values in \eqref{completed-tensor}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $\beta$, any $N\geq\htop(\beta)$ shows that $f_\beta$ is an
isomorphism. Take the product of these maps and of their inverses.
Both are continuous for the height topology. For bialgebras, preservation
of the operations is the morphism assertion of Theorem~\ref{tower-theorem}.
For a product or a Lie bracket alone, the same finite sum in each output
charge gives its continuous extension and the comparison identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
If the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{lie-recognition} and
\eqref{dimensions} hold in every degree, then
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak n_+(A)}\cong\widehat L,\qquad
 \widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+(A))}\cong\widehat{U(L)}
\]
as complete Lie algebras and complete bialgebras, respectively.  The second
formula denotes the height completion of the enveloping algebra; it does
not assert that the enveloping functor commutes with inverse limits.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Compatible reverse quotients]             \label{complete-quotients}
Suppose that maps \eqref{rhoN} exist for every $N$ and, for $M\leq N$,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\leq M}(\rho_N)=\rho_M                              \label{rho-compatible}
\end{equation}
under the identifications \eqref{transition}.  Then inverse limits give a
continuous split epimorphism
\[
 \widehat\rho:\widehat{\mathcal H}\twoheadrightarrow
 \widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+(A))}
\]
whose compatible section is induced by the maps $\jmath_N\Phi_N$, and
\[
 \widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+(A))}
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \widehat{\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}}.
\]
If every $\rho_N$ is injective, the full completed map is an isomorphism.
If $\mathcal H$ is a graded bialgebra, the $x_i$ are primitive, and every
$\rho_N$ is a bialgebra morphism, these conclusions respect the continuous
bialgebra operations and the completed tensor product
\eqref{completed-tensor}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{rho-compatible} defines $\widehat\rho$ componentwise.
The sections $\jmath_N\Phi_N$ are compatible and satisfy
\eqref{splitPhi}, so their inverse limit is a section; no general
preservation of epimorphisms by inverse limits is needed.  Proposition
\ref{reverse-quotient} identifies every spherical window.
Theorem~\ref{tower-theorem} and Proposition~\ref{completion} transport
these compatible maps and identities to the continuous operations.  If every
$\rho_N$ is injective, every finite window and hence the inverse limit is
identified.
\end{proof}

The same conclusion holds for towers with supplied restriction
isomorphisms and compatible reverse maps: Theorem~\ref{tower-theorem}
reconstructs the graded objects first. An independently defined complete
topological or derived Hall carrier still requires an identification with
the specified product completion.

\begin{remark}[The completed current quotient]
\label{completed-current-quotient}
For the current algebra of Remark~\ref{current-quotient-example},
\[
 \widehat C=\prod_{n\geq0}C_{n\alpha},\qquad
 \widehat B=\kk[[e]],\qquad
 \widehat\rho\,\widehat\Phi=1_{\widehat B},\qquad
 \ker\widehat\rho=\prod_{n\geq0}(I_C)_{n\alpha}.
\]
The maps are the products of the homogeneous maps in
\eqref{current-retraction}, so they are continuous Hopf maps for
\eqref{completed-tensor}. In each fixed charge an element of $\widehat C$
is still a finite polynomial sum; the completion permits infinitely many
charges, not an infinite sum of the generators in charge $\alpha$.

The completed kernel is the height-closed ideal generated by
$e_1,e_2,\ldots$. Indeed, its finite charge sums lie in $I_C$ and are
dense in the displayed product; the product is closed as the kernel of
the continuous map $\widehat\rho$ to a separated space.
This need not equal the algebraic ideal of finite sums of multiples of
these generators inside $\widehat C$. For instance,
$\sum_{n\geq1}e_n^n$ lies in the closed kernel. If it were a finite sum
of multiples of the $e_j$, setting those finitely many $e_j$ to zero
would annihilate it, while leaving some $e_n^n$ nonzero. Such evaluation
is well defined in every charge, giving a contradiction.

There is also a finite comparison in the ordinary rectangular tensor
that detects the chosen kernel. Put $B_j=\tau_{\leq j}B$ and
$C_j=\tau_{\leq j}C$. For output bounds $r=s=1$, the two identities are
\begin{align}
 (\rho_1\otimes\rho_1)\Delta^C_{1,1}
   &=\Delta^B_{1,1}\rho_2:C_2\longrightarrow B_1\otimes B_1,
   \label{current-rectangle}\\
 (\Phi_1\otimes\Phi_1)\Delta^B_{1,1}
   &=\Delta^C_{1,1}\Phi_2:B_2\longrightarrow C_1\otimes C_1.
   \notag
\end{align}
On the three charge-two monomials the first comparison is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 u&\Delta^C_{1,1}(u)&
 (\rho_1\otimes\rho_1)\Delta^C_{1,1}(u)&\Delta^B_{1,1}\rho_2(u)\\ \hline
 e_0^2&2e_0\otimes e_0&2e\otimes e&2e\otimes e\\
 e_0e_1&e_0\otimes e_1+e_1\otimes e_0&0&0\\
 e_1^2&2e_1\otimes e_1&0&0
\end{array}
\]
For the second comparison, both orders send $e^2$ to
$2e_0\otimes e_0$. The mixed monomial and the square of $e_1$ show
explicitly that the rectangular coproduct carries the chosen kernel
into terms killed by the retraction on an output factor.
The Hopf-map argument on all primitive generators proves these
identities in every charge; reconstruction extends them to the
completion. The three displayed monomials alone would not do so.
\end{remark}

\section{Compact realization and amplitude comparisons}
\label{sec:compact-realization}

The algebraic results identify the maps needed from the geometric
construction.
Theorem~\ref{gh:global-hopf} supplies the global normalized spherical
Hall/direct-sum comparison in its polynomial-crossing recipient, and
Corollary~\ref{gh:fibre-quotient} supplies its ordinary one-generator
fibre quotient. Its kernel is nonzero in every positive charge.
Theorems~\ref{sc:ordered-equivalence}, \ref{sc:coalgebra}, and
\ref{sh:hopf} construct the coefficient comparison, actual
direct-sum coproduct, and Springer Hopf lift over support.
Corollary~\ref{sh:global-crossing} identifies their global image.
Theorem~\ref{gh:support-family} compares their proper and
strict-splitting evaluations on the cyclic family, while
Theorem~\ref{cf:no-trace} still excludes a proper-trace extension
to the strict-splitting coefficient.
The full Hall product cannot be transported through an
ordinary-symmetry bialgebra identification by
Proposition~\ref{gh:ordinary-obstruction}.
For a broader Borcherds datum, the following requirements concern
additional classes and maps.
Relations first give the enveloping-to-spherical map $\Phi$.
A reverse quotient $\rho_N$ is additional data.
Its kernel measures the part of the finite Hall window outside the
recognized spherical subalgebra.
Theorem~\ref{sp:cohomology} identifies primitive cohomology only after
a differential graded Hopf comparison has been supplied.
For nonabelian primitive recognition,
Proposition~\ref{primitive-pairing} prevents the restriction of the same
normalized ambient pairing from being a nondegenerate Lie form.
Theorem~\ref{ce:uc:universal} supplies an algebraic full-current extension;
its central directions still require an action on states.

For translation-quotient sectors beyond the normalized construction of
Section~\ref{sec:spherical-hall}, a coefficient realization must apply
to \eqref{relative-hallcorr} and its unit $BE$.
The chosen reduced complexes need a determinant identity and coherent
square-root maps analogous to \eqref{orientation-cross-map}, including
their comparison with the unreduced cross line and the flag coherences.
Proposition~\ref{one-charge-associator} normalizes a scalar associator
only after its descent and cocycle hypotheses hold.
The explicit roots in Proposition~\ref{gh:roots} satisfy these
requirements on the smooth spherical locus; they do not extend them
to an arbitrary sheaf locus.
The following problem retains the broader compact comparison.

\begin{problem}[Compact Hall--Borcherds comparison]
For a smooth projective Calabi--Yau threefold and a specified
extension-closed sector, construct strong orientation data and the
associative product \eqref{hallproduct} in an explicit charge-twisted
monoidal realization, together with a strong monoidal totalization to the
ordinary $Q_+$-graded vector spaces of
Section~\ref{sec:associative-comparison}. For a bialgebra comparison this
passage must also preserve the relevant symmetry, or the ordinary
bialgebra interchange identity must be proved separately.
The super and cochain tensor constructions of
Section~\ref{sec:height-tensor} do not extend the ordinary recognition
theorem to odd or differential graded generators. In the resulting
degree-zero associative algebra, choose a finite multiplicity-one
Borcherds datum $A$ and classes
$x_i\in\mathcal H_{\alpha_i}$ of even cohomological parity.  With the
ordinary commutator $[x,y]=xy-yx$, prove the relations
\eqref{serre}--\eqref{orthogonal}.  This must first produce the surjection
\[
 \Phi:U(\mathfrak n_+(A))\twoheadrightarrow
       \mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}.
\]
Independently construct a Hall coproduct for which the $x_i$ are primitive
and, for every $N$, a graded bialgebra epimorphism
\begin{equation}
 \rho_N:\tau_{\leq N}\mathcal H\twoheadrightarrow
 \tau_{\leq N}U(\mathfrak n_+(A)),\qquad \rho_N(x_i)=e_i,    \label{open-rho}
\end{equation}
compatible as in \eqref{rho-compatible}.  Proposition
\ref{reverse-quotient} then identifies every spherical window without a
dimension calculation.  Determine $\ker\rho_N$.  If the intended target is
the full Hall algebra, prove $\ker\rho_N=0$ in every window; by Proposition
\ref{reverse-quotient}, this is equivalent to
$\tau_{\leq N}\mathcal H=\tau_{\leq N}\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}$.
Componentwise finite-dimensional equality with $B_N$ is one possible proof
of this last step.  Neither the scalar denominator nor the
opposite-direction map $\Phi_N$ constructs \eqref{open-rho}.

For a completed comparison, specify a separated complete Hall topology,
continuous product and coproduct, transition morphisms realizing every
finite window, and an identification of its completed tensor product with
\eqref{completed-tensor}. If the construction begins with finite windows,
supply the restriction isomorphisms of Theorem~\ref{tower-theorem}.
For ordinary rectangular outputs of bounds $r,s$, construct the map
with input bound $r+s$ in~\eqref{rectangular-coproduct}.
The compatible sections in
Proposition~\ref{complete-quotients}, rather than a general exactness claim
for inverse limits, then give the completed split epimorphism.  If the
carrier is derived, also identify it with the derived inverse limit and
justify the disappearance of higher inverse limits.  These requirements
are not supplied by a scalar denominator.
For $X=S\times E$ modulo translation, construct the further coefficients
and operations on the common-torsor quotient over $BE$.
Specify the realization of its unit and its relative tensor product.
Any passage to ordinary graded vector spaces must preserve these
operations. The absolute affine-stabilizer theorem does not supply
this passage. The normalized smooth spherical construction gives the
global polynomial-crossing comparison and its ordinary fibre quotient,
not a constant relative retraction or a full ordinary Hall bialgebra.
Its supported Springer crossing and Hall interchange are
Theorem~\ref{sh:hopf}, and its actual cyclic-family comparison is
Theorem~\ref{gh:support-family}. These use the normalized
spherical Betti coefficient; they do not construct interacting
critical coefficients or an independent field or Ran-space
realization.
\end{problem}

A representation introduces a further map
\[
 r:\mathcal H\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}(V),
\]
where the space $V$, its grading, and any completion must be specified.
The root operator is then $r(x_i)$.
Its algebra relations follow from a constructed algebra map $r$.
A scalar generating series does not specify this representation.

An amplitude also requires a trace or pairing on a declared domain.
For example, fix a subalgebra
$\mathcal D\subseteq\operatorname{End}(V)$ and a linear map
$\lambda:\mathcal D\to\Lambda$ to a one-dimensional value space.
If $\lambda$ is a trace, it must satisfy
$\lambda(ab)=\lambda(ba)$ on its domain.
For a graded trace, the corresponding Koszul sign is required.
The amplitude of an allowed Hall element $h$ is
$\lambda(r(h))$.
Infinite sums require a topology in which both the representation
and the trace are defined.

For an automorphic comparison in a parameter family $T$, the Hall
construction must produce a section $s_{\mathrm H}$ of a line bundle
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm H}$.
An independently defined automorphic section $s_{\mathrm{aut}}$
belongs to its own line bundle $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{aut}}$.
The required comparison is an isomorphism
\[
 \eta:\mathcal L_{\mathrm H}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
       \mathcal L_{\mathrm{aut}},
 \qquad \eta(s_{\mathrm H})=s_{\mathrm{aut}},
\]
with the specified group actions and normalization.
Finite-height recognition supplies neither this line isomorphism nor
the trace that defines $s_{\mathrm H}$.
