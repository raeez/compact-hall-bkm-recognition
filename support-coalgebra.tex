\section{The coefficient coalgebra over support}
\label{sec:support-coalgebra}

The support divisor records where a torsion sheaf is concentrated,
without requiring the sheaf to split. Its coefficient sheaf can therefore
retain a direct-sum coalgebra even though the strict decomposition of a
fixed cyclic module has the trace obstruction of
Theorem~\ref{cf:no-trace}. We construct this coalgebra from ordinary
Betti direct images. The essential step is an equivalence for the
actual external-product map, including coincident endpoint supports.

\subsection{Coefficients, supports, and the common base}

Continue with $\mathcal M_n=\mathfrak T_n(E)$ and
$B_E=BE$ from Section~\ref{sec:spherical-hall}. Put
\[
 C_n=\operatorname{Sym}^n(E),\qquad Y_n=[C_n/E],\qquad
 \overline c_n:\overline{\mathcal M}_n\longrightarrow Y_n,
 \qquad p_n:Y_n\longrightarrow B_E,
\]
where $\overline c_n$ is the Fitting-support morphism.
The normalization \eqref{gh:relative-coefficient} gives
\begin{equation}\label{sc:coefficients}
 \mathcal B_n=R\overline c_{n,*}\mathbb Q,
 \qquad \mathcal A_n=Rp_{n,*}\mathcal B_n.
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal A_n$ is the global relative coefficient object already
defined in Section~\ref{sec:spherical-hall}, since
$\pi_n=p_n\overline c_n$.
We work with Cartesian rational Betti complexes on the analytic
quotient stacks. They are bounded below and have finite-dimensional
cohomology in each degree on every multiplicity stratum of the support
atlas. The derived tensor uses direct-sum totalization. There is no
completion in Chern degree, and every $R*$ below is an ordinary direct
image. Our shift convention is $H^k(C[r])=H^{k+r}(C)$.
At charge zero,
\[
 \overline{\mathcal M}_0=Y_0=B_E,\qquad
 \mathcal B_0=\mathbb Q_{B_E}.
\]

For $a+b=n$ define
\[
 P_{a,b}=Y_a\times_{B_E}Y_b,\qquad
 S_{a,b}=\overline{\mathcal M}_a\times_{B_E}
                                      \overline{\mathcal M}_b.
\]
Let $\overline c_{a,b}:S_{a,b}\to P_{a,b}$ be the product of
support maps, $m_{a,b}:P_{a,b}\to Y_n$ addition of divisors,
and $s_{a,b}:S_{a,b}\to\overline{\mathcal M}_n$ direct sum.
These actual maps satisfy
\[
 \overline c_n s_{a,b}=m_{a,b}\overline c_{a,b}
\]
up to the natural isomorphism of families.
If $\operatorname{pr}_a,\operatorname{pr}_b$ are the projections
of $P_{a,b}$, write
\[
 \mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b
 =\operatorname{pr}_a^*\mathcal B_a
       \otimes^{\mathbb L}_{\mathbb Q_{P_{a,b}}}
                         \operatorname{pr}_b^*\mathcal B_b.
\]
The natural external-product transformation is
\begin{equation}\label{sc:kappa}
 \kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}:
 \mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b
       \longrightarrow R\overline c_{a,b,*}\mathbb Q.
\end{equation}
The superscript distinguishes it from the global map
$\kappa_{a,b}$ of \eqref{gh:direct-sum-map}.

\subsection{Matrix neighbourhoods and the external product}

\begin{lemma}\label{sc:neighbourhoods}
Before quotienting by $E$, let
$\xi=\sum_i m_i[x_i]\in C_n$, with distinct $x_i$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{sc:stalk-ring}
 H^*((Rc_{n,*}\mathbb Q)_\xi)
  =\bigotimes_i\mathbb Q[c_{i,1},\ldots,c_{i,m_i}],
 \qquad |c_{i,j}|=2j.
\end{equation}
The classes are the Chern classes of the spectral vector bundles
on actual neighbourhoods of $\xi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose pairwise disjoint coordinate disks $D_i$ about the $x_i$.
Translate and rescale their coordinates so that their images are
pairwise disjoint convex disks in $\mathbb C$, with centres
$\lambda_i$. The coordinate function on their disjoint union is
the holomorphic function used below. As the disks shrink, the sets
\[
 W=\prod_i\operatorname{Sym}^{m_i}(D_i)\subset C_n
\]
form cofinal neighbourhoods of $\xi$.
The inverse image $c_n^{-1}(W)$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{sc:matrix-stack}
 \prod_i[\operatorname{Mat}_{m_i}(D_i)/
                                      \operatorname{GL}_{m_i}(\mathbb C)],
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Mat}_m(D)$ consists of matrices whose eigenvalues
lie in the coordinate image of $D$.

To prove this equivalence on families, push a flat finite-length sheaf
along its finite support to the parameter space. The resulting vector
bundle has rank $n$. Multiplication by the coordinate function is an
endomorphism; a frame represents it by a matrix $A$. Conversely, the
presentation $\zeta I-A$ gives the sheaf. If the support lies in
several disks, the holomorphic Riesz projectors
\[
 P_i(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D_i}
                                  (\zeta I-A)^{-1}\,d\zeta
\]
split the vector bundle into spectral subbundles of ranks $m_i$.
They commute with frame changes and base change of the family.
Block direct sum and these projectors are inverse equivalences,
proving \eqref{sc:matrix-stack}, including nonsplit modules.

On the $i$th matrix space the homotopy
\[
 A\longmapsto\lambda_i I+t(A-\lambda_i I),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1,
\]
is equivariant for $\operatorname{GL}_{m_i}(\mathbb C)$ and stays
inside that space, by convexity of the disk. It contracts to the
scalar matrix $\lambda_i I$. The Borel realization of the quotient
therefore has homotopy type $B\operatorname{GL}_{m_i}(\mathbb C)$.
The equivalence forgets the matrix and retains its vector bundle,
and it is compatible with restriction to smaller concentric disks.

Now use $\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb C)\simeq U(m)$ and the
universal complex Grassmannian. Its rational cohomology is
$\mathbb Q[c_1,\ldots,c_m]$; restriction to a sum of lines sends
$c_j$ to the elementary symmetric polynomial $e_j$ in their
degree-two Chern classes. Its image is the symmetric-polynomial
ring. These characteristic-class facts follow from the even
Schubert cells and the splitting principle
\cite[Theorems~3.2,3.9]{Hatcher2017}.
Applying them on the cofinal neighbourhoods proves
\eqref{sc:stalk-ring}.
\end{proof}

The contraction in this proof need not preserve each Fitting divisor.
The proof computes ordinary images by neighbourhoods, and does not
replace a nonproper direct-image stalk by a strict-fibre cohomology
group.

\begin{theorem}\label{sc:external-product}
The map $\kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}$ is an equivalence for every
$a,b\geq0$. The corresponding external-product transformations for
any finite list of endpoints are equivalences, with their natural
associativity, permutation, and unit coherences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First work on $C_a\times C_b$. At a pair of divisors $(\xi,\eta)$,
use disks about the union of their support points, allowing zero
multiplicity for either endpoint. The inverse image of a product of
cofinal neighbourhoods has homotopy type
\[
 \prod_i\bigl(B\operatorname{GL}_{a_i}(\mathbb C)
                    \times B\operatorname{GL}_{b_i}(\mathbb C)\bigr).
\]
The two endpoint vector bundles remain independent even when their
supports coincide. In particular, the two factors cannot be replaced
by $B\operatorname{GL}_{a_i+b_i}(\mathbb C)$.

On these neighbourhoods the actual map
$\kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}$ is the cross product on cochains.
The spaces have nonnegative rational homology of finite dimension
in each degree. Eilenberg--Zilber and the field-coefficient
K\"unneth theorem therefore identify the cohomology of the product
in degree $k$ with the finite sum
$\bigoplus_{r+s=k}H^r\otimes H^s$.
In the Chern coordinates of \eqref{sc:stalk-ring}, the map is the
identity on the polynomial generators belonging to each endpoint.
It is an isomorphism in every degree. Cohomology stalks detect
equivalences, proving the assertion on the support atlas.

The descent to $B_E$ also requires a direct-image comparison.
The atlas of $Y_n$ is $C_n$, and the atlas of $P_{a,b}$ is
$C_a\times C_b$ with the diagonal $E$ action. The endpoints are
not quotiented independently. On the simplicial atlases the maps
are products of $E^k$ with the support maps. Locally on the smooth
$E^k$ factor, the inverse images of product neighbourhoods are a
contractible disk times the matrix-stack neighbourhoods above.
Their ordinary cochains have the same cohomology. This verifies
the Cartesian base-change transformations for these objects at
every simplicial level. It uses their actual neighbourhoods,
rather than a general nonproper base-change claim.

These calculations are uniformly bounded below, finite in each
degree, and compatible with the action maps because the external
product is natural. They descend to the asserted equivalence on
$P_{a,b}$. The same reasoning works for every finite list of
endpoints. The natural external-product transformations already
carry associativity, permutation, and unit coherences in the
derived enhancement. Inverting the equivalences preserves those
coherences; it does not require separate choices for different
parenthesizations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ordered supports and Chern roots}

Let
\[
 \widetilde Y_n=[E^n/E],\qquad
 q_n:\widetilde Y_n\longrightarrow Y_n
\]
be the finite map forgetting the order. Define
\[
 \widetilde L_n=[(E\times B\mathbb G_m)^n/E]
             =\widetilde Y_n\times(B\mathbb G_m)^n.
\]
Write $r_n:\widetilde L_n\to\widetilde Y_n$ for the projection
and $\sigma_n:\widetilde L_n\to\overline{\mathcal M}_n$
for the actual sum of the $n$ line skyscrapers. Set
\[
 \mathcal T_n=Rr_{n,*}\mathbb Q,\qquad
 \mathcal V_n=Rq_{n,*}\mathcal T_n.
\]
On the support atlas, $\mathcal T_n$ is the constant cochain
complex $C^*((B\mathbb G_m)^n;\mathbb Q)$, with its cup algebra
and permutation action. Product neighbourhoods prove this
constancy, and the coherent K\"unneth map compares it with
$C^*(B\mathbb G_m;\mathbb Q)^{\otimes n}$.
This notation retains the actual cochains; it makes no simultaneous
polynomial formality assertion for the operations over support.

Permuting the factors gives an $S_n$ action on $\mathcal V_n$.
It permutes the supports and the line factors, with the ordinary
Koszul signs on cochains. Direct sum is coherently unchanged by
these permutations. Its adjunction unit consequently gives
\begin{equation}\label{sc:theta}
 \theta_n:\mathcal B_n\longrightarrow
                              \mathcal V_n^{hS_n}.
\end{equation}
Over $\mathbb Q$, finite-group invariants are exact: averaging
splits the invariant subspace and higher group cohomology vanishes.
For the bounded-below complexes here this gives
$H^*(\mathcal V_n^{hS_n})=H^*(\mathcal V_n)^{S_n}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{sc:ordered-equivalence}
The actual map $\theta_n$ is an equivalence of the derived
coefficient complexes and of their rational cup algebras.
At $\xi=\sum_i m_i[x_i]$, it is the Chern-root map
\begin{equation}\label{sc:chern-roots}
 \bigotimes_i\mathbb Q[c_{i,1},\ldots,c_{i,m_i}]
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \mathbb Q[z_1,\ldots,z_n]^{\prod_iS_{m_i}},
 \qquad c_{i,j}\longmapsto e_j(z\text{ in cluster }i).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The orderings of $\xi$ form a transitive $S_n$-set with stabilizer
$H_\xi=\prod_iS_{m_i}$. Since $q_n$ is finite and proper, the
stalk of $\mathcal V_n$ is the direct sum over these orderings
of $C^*((B\mathbb G_m)^n;\mathbb Q)$.
Taking invariants identifies its cohomology with
$\mathbb Q[z_1,\ldots,z_n]^{H_\xi}$.
Lemma~\ref{sc:neighbourhoods} computes the source of $\theta_n$.
The ordered direct-sum map pulls each spectral vector bundle back
to the sum of its $m_i$ line bundles. Thus the map itself, rather
than merely its dimensions, is \eqref{sc:chern-roots}.
The symmetric-polynomial theorem makes it an isomorphism.
Stalkwise detection and the Cartesian descent established in
Theorem~\ref{sc:external-product} prove the equivalence on $Y_n$.
The construction uses pullback, ordinary direct image, and homotopy
fixed points of rational $E_\infty$ cup algebras, so it preserves
their cup-algebra structures as well.
\end{proof}

The cup algebra in this theorem is not the Hall product.
The proof shows that the stalk cohomology is even and finite in
each degree. The support space can still contribute odd global
cohomology. No support-level purity or smallness assertion has
been used to replace a sheaf by its cohomology.

\subsection{The supported coproduct and collisions}

Use the inverse from Theorem~\ref{sc:external-product} to define
\begin{equation}\label{sc:coproduct}
 \begin{split}
 \Delta^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}:\mathcal B_{a+b}
 &\longrightarrow Rm_{a,b,*}R\overline c_{a,b,*}\mathbb Q\\
 &\xrightarrow{\ Rm_{a,b,*}(\kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b})^{-1}\ }
       Rm_{a,b,*}(\mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The first arrow applies $R\overline c_{a+b,*}$ to the adjunction
unit $\mathbb Q\to Rs_{a,b,*}\mathbb Q$.

\begin{theorem}\label{sc:coalgebra}
The maps $\Delta^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}$ form a coherent coassociative
coalgebra for convolution by support addition. Their zero-endpoint
components are identity maps, and their counit is projection to
$\mathcal B_0=\mathbb Q_{B_E}$.
Let $H_{a,b}=S_a\times S_b$ act on the first $a$ and last $b$
ordered factors. Under the ordered equivalences there is a canonical
identification
\begin{equation}\label{sc:recipient}
 Rm_{a,b,*}(\mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b)
       \simeq \mathcal V_{a+b}^{hH_{a,b}},
\end{equation}
and $\Delta^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}$ is restriction of homotopy
fixed points from $S_{a+b}$ to $H_{a,b}$. There is no factorial
or Euler factor in this map.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Associativity of actual direct sum identifies both iterated units
with the unit for the sum of three endpoint sheaves. The coherent
external-product equivalences identify their common recipient as
\[
 Rm_{a,b,c,*}(\mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b
                                      \boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_c).
\]
The passage from iterated convolution to this recipient uses proper
base change for finite support addition. Applying the same argument
to every finite direct sum supplies coherent coassociativity.
If an endpoint is zero, direct sum is the identity and the external
product is the unit equivalence. This proves the identity components
and the counit statement.

Finite proper base change for $q_a\times_{B_E}q_b$, the
equivalences $\theta_a,\theta_b$, the constant line-cochain
K\"unneth map, and finite-group averaging give
\eqref{sc:recipient}. To identify the coproduct itself, compose
both proposed maps with the forgetful map to $\mathcal V_{a+b}$.
Both composites are pullback by the same ordered direct sum of
all $a+b$ line skyscrapers. Their identification is thus the natural
isomorphism of those direct-sum diagrams before taking invariants.
The invariant inclusion is a retract by averaging, so the diagram
restricts to its invariant factors. The same natural isomorphisms
commute with refinements of blocks. This proves the asserted map
and its coherences, with no change to the normalized coefficient.
\end{proof}

The stalk description fixes the collision maps explicitly.
At $\xi=\sum_i m_i[x_i]$, the finite fibre of $m_{a,b}$ is the
set of allocations
\[
 0\leq a_i\leq m_i,\qquad \sum_i a_i=a,\qquad b_i=m_i-a_i.
\]
The target stalk is the direct sum over these allocations of the
polynomial cohomology rings of
$\prod_i(B\operatorname{GL}_{a_i}\times B\operatorname{GL}_{b_i})$.
On the component for one allocation the actual map is
\begin{equation}\label{sc:whitney}
 c_{i,k}\longmapsto\sum_{r+s=k}c'_{i,r}c''_{i,s}.
\end{equation}
Here $c'_{i,0}=c''_{i,0}=1$, and classes above the relevant rank
vanish. This is the Whitney formula for that same direct-sum map.
For a cluster of length three, both iterated splittings to three
line endpoints give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_1&\longmapsto z_1+z_2+z_3,\\
 c_2&\longmapsto z_1z_2+z_1z_3+z_2z_3,\\
 c_3&\longmapsto z_1z_2z_3.
 \end{aligned}
\]
These $z_i$ remain line Chern classes, not support coordinates.

To describe the restriction across a support collision, split a
small disk into disjoint subdisks. The spectral vector bundle on
the original neighbourhood restricts to the sum of the subcluster
bundles. The resulting map on cohomology is again
\eqref{sc:whitney}; when all points separate, it is $c_k\mapsto e_k$.
Continuing along paths permutes the line or subcluster factors by
the actual ordered-cover monodromy. Thus \eqref{sc:theta} retains
both the finite permutation action and the collision restriction
maps. This is a statement about coefficient sheaves over support.
It does not apply proper base change to the nonproper strict
decomposition correspondence of Section~\ref{sec:companion-family}.

\subsection{Comparison with the global coproduct}

Let $p_{a,b}:P_{a,b}\to B_E$ be the structure map.
The external product for $p_a,p_b$ with the displayed coefficients is
\[
 \lambda_{a,b}:\mathcal A_a\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{\mathbb Q_{B_E}}
                  \mathcal A_b
       \longrightarrow
 Rp_{a,b,*}(\mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b).
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{sc:global-comparison}
The map $\lambda_{a,b}$ is an equivalence. Applying $Rp_{a+b,*}$
to $\Delta^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}$ and then $\lambda_{a,b}^{-1}$
gives exactly the global direct-sum coproduct
\eqref{gh:direct-sum-map}, with its finite-flag coherences.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The composite
$Rp_{a,b,*}\kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}\circ\lambda_{a,b}$
is the natural relative K\"unneth map for
$\pi_a=p_a\overline c_a$ and $\pi_b=p_b\overline c_b$.
Proposition~\ref{gh:direct-sum} proves that global map is an
equivalence on the actual common-torsor objects.
Theorem~\ref{sc:external-product} proves the same for
$\kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}$, so two-out-of-three proves the
assertion about $\lambda_{a,b}$.
Now $p_{a+b}m_{a,b}=p_{a,b}$. Both coproduct composites are
the adjunction unit for $s_{a,b}$ followed by the inverse of the
same global external product. They are therefore identified as
natural composites, and the identification commutes with every
finite refinement of direct sums.
\end{proof}

Pushing $\theta_n$ to $B_E$ similarly gives the ordered direct-sum
coefficient comparison of Proposition~\ref{gh:direct-sum}.
The simultaneous Hodge comparison of Theorem~\ref{gh:simultaneous}
then gives its polynomial action model. The common-base cochains
and the Koszul differential of \eqref{gh:koszul-model} are retained.
Neither a new global formality nor a support-level purity
assumption enters Proposition~\ref{sc:global-comparison}.

\subsection{The Hall product over support}

The Hall product over support uses the full extension stack.
For its endpoint map $p$ and proper middle-object map $q$, the
normalized coefficient map of Proposition~\ref{gh:coefficient-maps}
and the proper trace give the composite
\begin{equation}\label{sc:hall-product}
 \begin{split}
 Rm_{a,b,*}(\mathcal B_a\boxtimes_{B_E}\mathcal B_b)
 &\xrightarrow{\ Rm_{a,b,*}\kappa^{\mathrm{sup}}_{a,b}\ }
          Rm_{a,b,*}R\overline c_{a,b,*}\mathbb Q\\
 &\longrightarrow R\overline c_{a+b,*}Rq_*\mathbb Q
 \xrightarrow{\ R\overline c_{a+b,*}\operatorname{tr}_q\ }
          \mathcal B_{a+b}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The middle arrow is the endpoint adjunction unit followed by the
normalized coefficient map. This product is not cup product or
the trace of the finite ordered-support cover.

The next section compares this fixed product with
\eqref{sc:coproduct}. Proper full flags supply a Springer action
on the ordered coefficient $\mathcal V_n$ across support
collisions. Their trace identifies the product with a shuffle
sum, while the same inclusion of invariant objects identifies
the coproduct. This constructs the crossing over support without
deducing a sheaf map from global cohomology or from the scalar
flag degree. Theorem~\ref{gh:support-family} then compares its
proper and strict-splitting evaluations on the cyclic family,
retaining the trace obstruction of Theorem~\ref{cf:no-trace}.
