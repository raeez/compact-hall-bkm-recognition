\title{Finite-Height Positive Borcherds Recognition\\
and the Compact Hall Problem}
\author{Raeez Lorgat}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
A scalar denominator and the Hilbert series supplied by the
Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt theorem can agree for nonisomorphic graded Lie
algebras.  We exhibit this failure before fixing an integral symmetrizable
Borcherds--Cartan matrix and proving finite-height recognition of its purely
even positive Lie algebra.  The proof takes place in symmetric monoidal
height truncations.  We then isolate the additional surjection from an
enveloping algebra to an associatively generated Hall subalgebra and show
why Lie recognition alone does not make it injective.  For a smooth
projective Calabi--Yau threefold, the extension correspondence and
Donaldson--Thomas carrier are known, but the strong orientation coherence
needed for an associative product is not known in general.  The compact
Hall comparison, its bialgebra structure, and its completed realization
therefore remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{One scalar shadow, two brackets}

Throughout, $\kk$ is a field of characteristic zero.  For a positively
graded vector space $V=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}V_n$ with finite-dimensional
components, write
\[
 D_V(t)=\prod_{n\geq1}(1-t^n)^{\dim_\kk V_n}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[The scalar shadow is not faithful]
There are nonisomorphic positively graded Lie algebras $L$ and $L_0$ for
which
\[
 D_L(t)=D_{L_0}(t)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L)=\operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L_0).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $L$ have basis $x,y$ in degree one and $z$ in degree two, with
$[x,y]=z$ and $z$ central.  Let $L_0$ have the same graded vector space and
zero bracket.  Then
\[
 [L,L]=\kk z,\qquad [L_0,L_0]=0,
\]
so the two Lie algebras are not isomorphic.  Their common scalar
denominator is
\[
 D_L(t)=D_{L_0}(t)=(1-t)^2(1-t^2).
\]
The Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt theorem gives
\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L)
 =\operatorname{Hilb}_t U(L_0)
 =\frac{1}{(1-t)^2(1-t^2)}.
\]
Nevertheless, $U(L_0)$ is commutative, whereas $xy-yx=z\neq0$ in $U(L)$.
\end{proof}

Thus graded dimensions determine neither the Lie bracket nor the
associative product of its enveloping algebra.  They also do not determine
the correspondences, orientations, or functorial maps from which a Hall
product is formed.

\section{The positive Borcherds presentation}

Let $I$ be finite.  An \emph{integral symmetrizable
Borcherds--Cartan matrix} is an integral matrix
$A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $a_{ii}=2$ or $a_{ii}\leq0$;
\item $a_{ij}\leq0$ for $i\neq j$, and $a_{ij}=0$ if and only if
      $a_{ji}=0$;
\item there are positive integers $d_i$ for which
      $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$.
\end{enumerate}
Put
\[
 \Ire=\{i\in I:a_{ii}=2\},\qquad
 \Iim=\{i\in I:a_{ii}\leq0\}.
\]
The symmetric form on
$Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb Z\alpha_i$ is
$(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)=d_i a_{ij}$.  Thus the simple roots indexed by
$\Ire$ have positive square and those indexed by $\Iim$ have nonpositive
square.  We work in ordinary vector spaces: every simple generator is even,
and no Lie-superalgebra signs occur.

Write $Q_+=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\alpha_i$ and
\[
 \htop\left(\sum_i n_i\alpha_i\right)=\sum_i n_i.
\]
Let $\mathfrak F$ be the free $Q_+$-graded Lie algebra on generators $e_i$
of degree $\alpha_i$.  Let $R_A\subset\mathfrak F$ be the homogeneous ideal
generated by
\begin{align}
 (\operatorname{ad}e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j&=0
 &&(i\in\Ire,\ j\neq i),                                    \label{serre}\\
 [e_i,e_j]&=0
 &&(i\neq j,\ a_{ij}=0).                                    \label{orthogonal}
\end{align}
The positive Borcherds Lie algebra considered here is
\[
 \mathfrak n_+(A)=\mathfrak F/R_A.
\]
These are the normalized positive relations in Borcherds'
presentation~\cite[\S1]{Borcherds1988}.  A simple imaginary generator
$e_i$, $i\in\Iim$, imposes no relation
$(\operatorname{ad}e_i)^m e_j=0$ unless the orthogonality relation
\eqref{orthogonal} applies.  A relation based at a real index $j$ may still
constrain $e_i$.  For example, for
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
the relation based at $1$ is
$(\operatorname{ad}e_1)^2e_2=0$, and there is no converse Serre relation
based at $2$.

Finiteness of $I$ implies that every $Q_+$-homogeneous component of
$\mathfrak F$, and hence of $\mathfrak n_+(A)$, is finite-dimensional.

\begin{remark}[What the positive presentation does not recover]
The matrix $A$ is fixed input.  Its positive Lie algebra need not determine
$A$: the rank-one matrices $(0)$ and $(-2)$ both give
$\mathfrak n_+=\kk e$.  Recognition of $\mathfrak n_+(A)$ therefore does
not recover the Cartan matrix, the Cartan and negative summands, or the full
Borcherds algebra.  The present formulation also assumes a finite set of
independent simple charges, one even generator for each charge.  Charged
generator spaces, odd simple roots, and Borcherds--Bozec generators in
multiple degrees are outside its scope.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite windows with all operations typed}

For $N\geq1$, let $\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$ be the category of
$Q_+$-graded $\kk$-vector spaces supported in heights at most $N$.  Its
tensor product is
\[
 (V\mathbin{\bar\otimes_N}W)_\beta
   =\bigoplus_{\gamma+\delta=\beta}V_\gamma\otimes W_\delta,
 \qquad \htop(\beta)\leq N.
\]
With the usual symmetry this is a symmetric monoidal category.  The functor
\[
 \tau_{\leq N}V=\bigoplus_{\htop(\beta)\leq N}V_\beta
\]
from nonnegatively $Q_+$-graded vector spaces to
$\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$ is strong symmetric monoidal.  Indeed, if
$\beta=\gamma+\delta$ and $\htop(\beta)\leq N$, then both $\gamma$ and
$\delta$ have height at most $N$.  It therefore carries Lie algebras,
associative algebras, coalgebras, and bialgebras to objects of the same kind.
For $M\leq N$,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\leq M}\tau_{\leq N}=\tau_{\leq M}.                  \label{transition}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\tau_{\leq N}U(\mathfrak g)$ is a bialgebra object in the
truncated category.  The ordinary algebra quotient obtained by killing
heights above $N$ need not be an ordinary coalgebra quotient.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-height Lie recognition]                \label{lie-recognition}
Let
\[
 L=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}}L_\beta
\]
be a $Q_+$-graded Lie algebra with finite-dimensional homogeneous pieces.
Suppose that elements $x_i\in L_{\alpha_i}$ generate $L$ and satisfy
\eqref{serre} and \eqref{orthogonal}, with $e_i$ replaced by $x_i$.
Then there is a unique surjective graded Lie homomorphism
\begin{equation}
 \pi:\mathfrak n_+(A)\longrightarrow L,\qquad e_i\longmapsto x_i.
                                                               \label{pi}
\end{equation}
If, for every $\beta\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$ with
$\htop(\beta)\leq N$,
\begin{equation}
 \dim_\kk L_\beta=\dim_\kk\mathfrak n_+(A)_\beta,             \label{dimensions}
\end{equation}
then
\[
 \tau_{\leq N}\pi:\tau_{\leq N}\mathfrak n_+(A)
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\tau_{\leq N}L
\]
is an isomorphism of Lie algebra objects in
$\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\leq N}U(\pi):\tau_{\leq N}U(\mathfrak n_+(A))
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\tau_{\leq N}U(L)               \label{pbwiso}
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism of bialgebra objects in that category.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assignment $e_i\mapsto x_i$ gives a surjection
$\mathfrak F\twoheadrightarrow L$.  The displayed relations place $R_A$ in
its kernel, so it factors through the unique surjection \eqref{pi}.  For
each $\beta$ of height at most $N$, the map $\pi_\beta$ is a surjection
between finite-dimensional spaces of the same dimension and hence is an
isomorphism.

Give both enveloping algebras the PBW filtration.  The associated graded map
of $U(\pi)$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}(\pi):
 \operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak n_+(A))\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Sym}(L).
\]
In a $Q_+$-degree $\beta$ of height at most $N$, every symmetric monomial
uses only Lie components of height at most $N$; hence
$\operatorname{Sym}(\pi)_\beta$ is an isomorphism.  The PBW filtration in a
fixed positive height is finite, so $U(\pi)_\beta$ is an isomorphism.
Functoriality of the standard primitive coproduct makes $U(\pi)$ a
bialgebra map, and strong monoidality of $\tau_{\leq N}$ gives
\eqref{pbwiso}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The dimensions in \eqref{dimensions} do not create a bracket.  The
relations and the chosen generators first produce the surjection \eqref{pi};
the dimensions only show that its kernel vanishes through height $N$.
\end{remark}

\section{The additional associative comparison}

Let $\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_+}\mathcal H_\beta$ be a unital
$Q_+$-graded associative algebra, and let
$x_i\in\mathcal H_{\alpha_i}$.  Denote by $L\subset\mathcal H$ the Lie
subalgebra generated by the $x_i$ under the commutator, and by
$\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}\subset\mathcal H$ the unital associative
subalgebra generated by the same elements.  The inclusion
$L\hookrightarrow\mathcal H$ induces an algebra map
\[
 U(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}},
\]
but Lie recognition alone says nothing about its kernel.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-height associative recognition]    \label{associative-recognition}
If the $x_i$ satisfy \eqref{serre} and \eqref{orthogonal}, there is a unique
surjective graded algebra map
\begin{equation}
 \Phi:U(\mathfrak n_+(A))\twoheadrightarrow
       \mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}},\qquad e_i\longmapsto x_i.    \label{Phi}
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}
 \dim_\kk\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}_\beta
 =\dim_\kk U(\mathfrak n_+(A))_\beta
 \qquad\bigl(\htop(\beta)\leq N\bigr),                        \label{associative-dimensions}
\end{equation}
then $\tau_{\leq N}\Phi$ is an isomorphism of associative algebra objects
in $\mathsf{Gr}_{\leq N}(Q_+)$.  If $\mathcal H$ is a graded bialgebra and
the $x_i$ are primitive, then $\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}$ is a graded
sub-bialgebra, $\Phi$ is a bialgebra map, and the same conclusion holds for
bialgebra objects.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relations give a Lie map
$\mathfrak n_+(A)\to\mathcal H$ with image $L$.  Its enveloping map has
image exactly the unital algebra generated by the $x_i$, proving
\eqref{Phi}.  Under \eqref{associative-dimensions}, every homogeneous
component through height $N$ is a surjection between finite-dimensional
spaces of equal dimension.  If the $x_i$ are primitive, the standard
coproduct on $U(\mathfrak n_+(A))$ and the coproduct on $\mathcal H$ agree
on the algebra generators, which proves the bialgebra assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Lie PBW is not Hall PBW]
For $A=(0)$, take
\[
 \mathcal H=\kk[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 \deg\varepsilon=\alpha,\qquad x=\varepsilon.
\]
Then $L=\kk\varepsilon\cong\mathfrak n_+(A)$, so
Theorem~\ref{lie-recognition} recognizes the Lie algebra in every height.
Nevertheless,
\[
 U(\mathfrak n_+(A))=\kk[e]\longrightarrow\mathcal H,\qquad
 e\longmapsto\varepsilon
\]
has kernel $(e^2)$.  Thus \eqref{dimensions} cannot replace the
associative dimension comparison \eqref{associative-dimensions}.  To
recognize all of $\mathcal H$, one must also prove
$\mathcal H=\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}$; this fails whenever, for example,
$\mathcal H_0$ contains more than the unit.
\end{remark}

\section{What compatible finite windows imply}

For a nonnegatively $Q_+$-graded vector space $V$, set
\[
 F^{N+1}V=\bigoplus_{\htop(\beta)>N}V_\beta,\qquad
 \widehat V=\varprojlim_N V/F^{N+1}V
            =\prod_{\beta\in Q_+}V_\beta.
\]
The transition maps are the projections in \eqref{transition}.  The tensor
product appropriate to this topology is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V\mathbin{\widehat\otimes_{\mathrm{ht}}}\widehat W
 =
 \prod_{\beta\in Q_+}
 \left(\bigoplus_{\gamma+\delta=\beta}
 V_\gamma\otimes W_\delta\right).                            \label{completed-tensor}
\end{equation}
Each inner sum is finite because $I$ is finite.

\begin{proposition}[Formal height completion]                 \label{completion}
Let $f:V\to W$ be a graded linear map.  If
$\tau_{\leq N}f$ is an isomorphism for every $N$, then $f$ is a
componentwise isomorphism and extends to a continuous isomorphism
\[
 \widehat f:\widehat V\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\widehat W.
\]
If $V$ and $W$ are graded Lie algebras, associative algebras, or bialgebras
and $f$ is a morphism of the corresponding kind, then $\widehat f$ respects
the continuous operations; for bialgebras the completed coproduct takes
values in \eqref{completed-tensor}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $\beta$, any $N\geq\htop(\beta)$ shows that $f_\beta$ is an
isomorphism.  Taking the product of the maps $f_\beta$ gives
$\widehat f$.  A graded product, bracket, or coproduct has only finitely
many summands in each fixed total degree, so its continuous extension is
defined componentwise and is preserved by $\widehat f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
If the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{lie-recognition} and
\eqref{dimensions} hold in every degree, then
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak n_+(A)}\cong\widehat L,\qquad
 \widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+(A))}\cong\widehat{U(L)}
\]
as complete Lie algebras and complete bialgebras, respectively.  The second
formula denotes the height completion of the enveloping algebra; it does
not assert that the enveloping functor commutes with inverse limits.
\end{corollary}

This formal conclusion applies to a single graded comparison, or to a
compatible inverse system already known to arise from such graded objects.
It does not identify an independently defined topological or derived Hall
carrier with that inverse limit.

\section{The compact Hall boundary}

Let $X$ be a smooth projective complex Calabi--Yau threefold.  Choose an
extension-closed subcategory $\mathcal A\subset\operatorname{Coh}(X)$,
closed under direct sums, whose objects form an open derived substack of
the derived moduli stack of perfect complexes on $X$.  Choose a charge map
\[
 \operatorname{cl}:K_0(\mathcal A)\longrightarrow Q
\]
which is locally constant in families and whose effective classes lie in
$Q_+$.  Assume that the Euler form factors through an antisymmetric
bilinear form $\chi:Q\times Q\to\mathbb Z$:
\[
 \chi(\operatorname{cl}E,\operatorname{cl}F)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^r
   \dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Ext}^r(E,F).
\]
Let $\mathfrak M_\beta$ be the open-and-closed derived substack of objects
of charge $\beta$.  With the convention that the first factor is the
subobject, the stack
$\mathfrak E_{\beta,\gamma}$ of short exact sequences
\[
 0\longrightarrow E_\beta\longrightarrow E
  \longrightarrow E_\gamma\longrightarrow0
\]
has the typed correspondence
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak M_\beta\times\mathfrak M_\gamma
 \xleftarrow{\ p_{\beta,\gamma}\ }
 \mathfrak E_{\beta,\gamma}
 \xrightarrow{\ q_{\beta,\gamma}\ }
 \mathfrak M_{\beta+\gamma}.                                 \label{hallcorr}
\end{equation}

The $(-1)$-shifted symplectic structure supplies, after a choice of
orientation $o$, Donaldson--Thomas coefficient complexes
$\mathcal P_{\beta,o}$.  A prospective cohomological carrier is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_\beta
   =\mathbb H^\bullet(\mathfrak M_\beta,\mathcal P_{\beta,o}),
 \qquad
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_+}\mathcal H_\beta.         \label{hallcarrier}
\end{equation}
For a moduli-like stack whose classical truncation is a quasi-separated
Artin stack with affine stabilizers and is $\Theta$-reductive, and for which
the unit inclusion is closed, a \emph{strong orientation datum} contains
the compatibility with direct sums and the coherence for triple direct
sums.  It yields maps of the form
\begin{equation}
 m_{\beta,\gamma}:
 \mathcal H_\beta\otimes\mathcal H_\gamma
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal H_{\beta+\gamma}[\chi(\beta,\gamma)],               \label{hallproduct}
\end{equation}
where $\chi$ is the antisymmetric Euler pairing.  The shift in
\eqref{hallproduct} is part of the structure: componentwise shifts cannot
remove a nonzero antisymmetric bilinear degree.  Indeed, replacing
$\mathcal H_\beta$ by $\mathcal H_\beta[s(\beta)]$ changes the degree to
\[
 \chi_s(\beta,\gamma)=\chi(\beta,\gamma)+s(\beta)+s(\gamma)
 -s(\beta+\gamma),\qquad
 \chi_s(\beta,\gamma)-\chi_s(\gamma,\beta)=2\chi(\beta,\gamma).
\]
Thus the product must be placed in its specified charge-twisted monoidal
category.

Kontsevich and Soibelman construct this structure for quivers with
potential~\cite[\S7]{KontsevichSoibelman2011}.  In the general
three-Calabi--Yau setting, the hypotheses above and strong orientation data
give associativity
by~\cite[Theorem A, Definition 8.4, Corollary 8.8]{KinjoParkSafronov2026}.
Joyce and Upmeier construct canonical orientation data for smooth
projective Calabi--Yau threefolds~\cite[Theorem 4.4]{JoyceUpmeier2021}.
They identify the obstruction to a strong lift as a class in
$H^3(K^{\mathrm{semi}}_0(X),\mathbb Z/2)$ and leave its vanishing
open~\cite[Theorem 3.10, Question 3.11]{JoyceUpmeier2021}.
Without that coherence, the product is associative only up to choices of
sign~\cite[Remark 8.7]{KinjoParkSafronov2026}; the compactly supported
construction of~\cite[Theorem 7.22]{Descombes2025} likewise assumes a
strong orientation datum and records the unresolved lift in
\cite[Remark 7.23]{Descombes2025}.  Thus
\eqref{hallcorr} and \eqref{hallcarrier} do not yet furnish the required
associative compact Hall algebra.

\begin{remark}
For the two-Calabi--Yau category of semistable coherent sheaves of fixed
slope on a projective K3 surface, the $\psi$-sign-twisted BPS algebra is an
enveloping algebra of a generalized Kac--Moody Lie algebra
~\cite[\S6.3, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}.
This is a compact two-Calabi--Yau theorem with graded generator spaces.
The BPS algebra embeds in the full cohomological Hall algebra
~\cite[Proposition 6.4]{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}, and twisting
both multiplications preserves this inclusion.  After choosing a positive
determinant line bundle, the PBW comparisons for the full algebra are not
algebra morphisms
~\cite[Theorem 1.7, Corollary 1.8, and the following paragraph]
{DavisonHennecartSchlegelMejia2025}; they therefore do not supply the
three-Calabi--Yau algebra or bialgebra surjection required above.
\end{remark}

\begin{problem}[Compact Hall--Borcherds comparison]
For a smooth projective Calabi--Yau threefold and a specified
extension-closed sector, construct strong orientation data and the
associative product \eqref{hallproduct} in an explicit charge-twisted
monoidal realization, together with a strong monoidal totalization to the
ordinary $Q_+$-graded vector spaces of Section~4.  In the resulting
degree-zero associative algebra, choose a finite multiplicity-one
Borcherds datum $A$ and classes
$x_i\in\mathcal H_{\alpha_i}$ of even cohomological parity.  With the
ordinary commutator $[x,y]=xy-yx$, prove the relations
\eqref{serre}--\eqref{orthogonal}.  This must first produce the surjection
\[
 \Phi:U(\mathfrak n_+(A))\twoheadrightarrow
       \mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}.
\]
Prove \eqref{associative-dimensions} in every finite height, and prove
$\mathcal H=\mathcal H^{\mathrm{sph}}$ if the intended target is the full
Hall algebra.  A bialgebra comparison additionally requires a Hall
coproduct for which the $x_i$ are primitive.

For a completed comparison, specify a separated complete Hall topology,
continuous product and coproduct, transition morphisms realizing every
finite window, and an identification of its completed tensor product with
\eqref{completed-tensor}.  If the carrier is derived, also identify it with
the derived inverse limit and justify the disappearance of higher inverse
limits.  None of these comparison maps or completion statements is supplied
by a scalar denominator.
\end{problem}
